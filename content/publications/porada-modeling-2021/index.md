abstract: Understanding natural language requires common sense, one aspect of which
  is the ability to discern the plausibility of events. While distributional models—most
  recently pre-trained, Transformer language models—have demonstrated improvements
  in modeling event plausibility, their performance still falls short of humans'.
  In this work, we show that Transformer-based plausibility models are markedly inconsistent
  across the conceptual classes of a lexical hierarchy, inferring that “a person breathing”
  is plausible while “a dentist breathing” is not, for example. We find this inconsistency
  persists even when models are softly injected with lexical knowledge, and we present
  a simple post-hoc method of forcing model consistency that improves correlation
  with human plausibility judgements.
